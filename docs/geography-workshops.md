# Geography workshops — 29 September 2026

All **86 Geography teaching/review sessions** are revised: 28 for 7A and 58 for 7B. The ten Geography assessments remain assessments. Class assignments, lesson IDs, timetable positions and geog.1 section references remain available.

## Teaching without books

Students need notebooks and pencils. A ruler helps with drawing tasks; supplied measurements allow calculation without measuring a projected image. **No textbook, worksheet, atlas or student internet connection is required for the core tasks.** The equipment stations include a mini atlas index instead of requiring an atlas.

| Stage | Minutes | Teacher action / student outcome |
|---|---:|---|
| A problem to solve | 4 | Take a prediction vote and hear provisional reasons. |
| Teach and demonstrate | 10 | Explain terms and mechanisms using the mission's authored worked example; read supplied sources. |
| Class board challenge | 8 | Predict, tap, explain, check and retry; use the optional model if helpful. |
| Solve a new task | 12 | Produce a map, calculation, explanation, design or decision. **Show task source** keeps the information accessible. |
| Check the reasoning | 4 | Hear answers before revealing a worked response. |
| Independent exit | 2 | Collect a short independent explanation or calculation. |

7A weeks sometimes combine two original plan rows. They receive distinct missions, with teaching and simulations shared where the topic is the same. Applied sessions change the problem or evidence; reviews use diagnostic stations. No visual is repeated in a later stage merely to fill time.

## Start with 7A Lesson 2

`g7a-geo-w02-1` explicitly explains a **link**: two things affect or depend on one another. It contrasts a list (“river, bridge”) with a mechanism (“the bridge crosses the river, so people can reach the other bank”). Students complete a guided sentence before creating and explaining their own labelled connection. There is no required book exercise.

## Investigations

Fullscreen SVG models cover 37 types. Quantitative controls include rainfall/infiltration/runoff, ice additions/losses, map length/scale and tenths within a grid square. Other models have three comparison scenes showing changes in landforms, layers, routes, patterns or evidence. Flow animations are teacher-controlled and respect reduced motion; static models do not show a nonfunctional Play button.

Every teaching/review session also has **Open board challenge**. Six touch/keyboard formats cover sorting, sequencing, decisions with changed cases, plotting a grid location, allocating water and choosing a flood-management package. Controls are at least 56 pixels high, require no dragging or hover, and provide Check, explanation, retry and Reset. The teacher can tap for the class, or invite a pupil. All pupils predict before the tap; a second pupil explains the reason. Choices persist when reopening during the page session but are not saved as pupil grades.

The runoff model conserves its simplified water budget but omits storage, evaporation and routing. Glacier mass balance does not predict real annual snout displacement. Most landscapes are schematics, not measured reconstructions. The UK model retains geographical country outlines. Numerical cases are classroom examples, not national statistics.

## Alignment and limits

The plans supply section codes and objectives; the complete Geography textbook pages were not supplied. This revision follows those topics and objectives, **not a verified transcription of every printed exercise**. No printed page numbers are invented.

The six-landform source covers waterfalls, gorges, meanders, oxbow lakes, floodplains and natural levees. The exact six selected by the physical book should be checked when its pages are available. The Thames lesson separates a fictional flood log from the factual explanation of the Thames Barrier; invented figures are not reported as historical Thames data.

The classroom player is updated. Existing multilingual independent-study exports under `students/` have not been regenerated in this teacher-led redesign.

## Distinct lesson progression
The 7B Week 2 pair now separates UK location (Deliver four postcards) from journey planning (Plan an Irish Sea journey). The latter teaches connected travel legs, waiting time, budget constraints and revising an infeasible plan, with a dedicated SVG comparison and new exit/practice questions. Figures are fictional classroom data. Section 3.1 remains the location anchor; the journey is a teacher-created application, not a claimed book exercise.

Across Geography, 100 mission-specific demonstrations replace generic follow-on guidance. There are 58 authored changed cases for applied work. Explicit review sessions revisit earlier ideas through mistake clinics, evidence hearings, teaching a missing step, counterexamples and field-note repair. Regression checks include review tasks when detecting duplicated sequences. These checks cannot establish enjoyment or replace classroom feedback.
