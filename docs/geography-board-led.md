# Geography: teach from the board

All 86 Geography teaching and review lessons now use the board-led route. The 42 scheduled topics retain their section links, lesson IDs and case evidence. The ten assessments retain their independent conditions.

Open a lesson normally. Teach the explanation beside its map or illustration. **Read teaching script** is directly visible on every screen; it opens the complete explanation in large readable text. The [full teaching guide](geography-teacher-guide.html) provides the same scripts in one place and can print a selected lesson.

The route introduces a geographical question, explains words and concepts, demonstrates the method, explains the scheduled case and a changed example, checks briefly through spoken answers and recaps the idea. Students need no notebooks, devices or partner tasks to follow it. The optional lesson sheet uses the actual case source; optional homework is separate consolidation. Neither is needed during the teaching route.

Planned timing: introduction 3 minutes, goal 2, vocabulary 3, explanation 17, worked examples 7, explained cases 4, spoken checks 3, recap 1. Combined lessons divide those blocks between their topics. These are planning estimates, not measured classroom durations. Teachers should adjust pace to the class.

## What changed

The previous final course conversion imposed independent investigations, products and revision tasks on top of the teaching content. The final conversion now creates the teacher-led route instead. Forty-two concrete topic explanations add connected examples, calculations and common misconceptions. Full subject explanations remain separate from concise board text.

Three new illustrations teach repeated observation, route diversion after a bridge closure, and downhill glacier movement during snout retreat. They join the existing original geography artwork, accurate country outlines and labelled numerical diagrams. Imagined landscapes and numerical teaching models are identified as such; they are not observations of named places.

## Reference checks

- [Ordnance Survey: map-reading guide](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/resources/map-reading.pdf): map scale, grid references and contours.
- [Met Office: UK rainfall](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/types-of-weather/rain/how-much-does-it-rain-in-the-uk): relief and rainfall.
- [Met Office: climate](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/climate): long-term weather patterns.
- [USGS: glacier change and mass balance](https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2009/3046/): changes in glacier mass and extent.
- [British Geological Survey: erosion](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/discovering-geology/geological-processes/erosion/): erosion processes.

These references support subject checks, rather than supplying copied lesson text. All new numerical examples are invented classroom models. Image prompts and generation provenance are recorded in assets/geography/board-lecture-art-prompts.json. The town illustration was edited to make the closed bridge visibly connect both banks.
