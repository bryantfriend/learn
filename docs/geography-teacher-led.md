# Teacher-led Geography — 2 October 2026

Historical description of the earlier six-section route. The current lessons follow the [eight-section concept-screen redesign](geography-concepts.md), approved on 6 October 2026.

Replaces the pupil-work-heavy core of all 86 Geography teaching and review sessions for 7A and 7B. The ten Geography assessments retain their assessment conditions. Lesson IDs, scheduled positions, section references and mission-specific examples remain intact.

The teacher’s classroom report was that the illustrated Irish Sea journey lesson lasted approximately ten minutes. A sum of suggested slide timings had been mistaken for sufficient instructional content. The revised teaching materials address the explanations and examples rather than extending the timers.

## Classroom format

The actual period is 40 minutes. Pupils are ESL learners. They remain seated and listen to teacher explanations, give occasional short answers or opinions, and may be invited individually to a board demonstration. The core requires no notebooks, printing or pupil devices.

| Time | Teaching |
|---|---|
| 0–3 | Introduce the topic and recall a relevant idea. |
| 3–25 | Explain subject concepts with diagrams, define vocabulary and work through the lesson’s example. |
| 25–31 | Demonstrate the board activity and a second sourced example; an occasional volunteer turn is optional. |
| 31–36 | Explain the changed example and its consequences. |
| 36–39 | Take brief factual responses and one opinion about priorities; correct misconceptions. |
| 39–40 | Give the spoken exit prompt and dismiss. |

These are teaching estimates. The revised route has not been observed for a full period in the actual classroom. Its operational and content checks do not certify teaching duration or comprehension.

## What was rebuilt

Every one of the 42 Geography sections has at least four authored concept explanations, short pupil-facing lines, a brief oral question and an expected answer. Additional topic explanations restore subject knowledge named in the school plans, including Ironbridge, map development, UK population and culture, London, international links, glacier locations and the Thames Estuary. Worked demonstrations retain the method and figures specific to each scheduled mission. Review sessions revisit selected concepts using their separate case sources. Combined timetable sessions retain each distinct mission.

The classroom player provides **Teaching notes for this slide** and **Brief understanding check**. Explanations for oral checks remain concealed until opened. Stage notes and the teacher-led guide contain the complete sequence. Teacher notes are visible on the shared classroom display; prepare from the separate guide if you prefer to keep explanatory notes off that screen.

The printable [teacher guide](geography-teacher-guide.html) contains all 86 lessons. Select a lesson to read or print it. Regenerate with `node scripts/build-geography-teacher-guide.mjs` after changing the authored curriculum.

Section 3.1 now teaches island versus country, UK versus Great Britain, relative location and sea connections before journey planning. Its separate worked example uses 1 + 2 + 2 = 5 hours; its main ferry/flight case uses its own 7-hour/35-token and 6-hour/60-token figures. All four previously omitted mission explanations now appear in the actual teaching route. The existing generated pictures support the teacher’s demonstration.

## Sources and interpretation

Section sequence and objectives come from the existing school plans. Complete geog.1 textbook pages were not supplied; these are original explanations aligned to the recorded topics, rather than verified reproductions of textbook exercises. Classroom quantitative cases are explicitly fictional.

Subject reference checks use [Ordnance Survey map reading](https://www.ordnancesurvey.co.uk/documents/resources/map-reading.pdf), [Met Office rainfall explanations](https://weather.metoffice.gov.uk/learn-about/weather/types-of-weather/rain/how-much-does-it-rain-in-the-uk), [USGS water cycle](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/water-science-school/water-cycle), [USGS glacier terminology](https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/2004/1216/text.html), [BGS British glacial history](https://www.bgs.ac.uk/discovering-geology/fossils-and-geological-time/geological-timechart/), [UNESCO Ironbridge Gorge](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/371), and [Environment Agency Thames Barrier guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-thames-barrier).
