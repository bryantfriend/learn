## Classroom adaptation

Each teaching session includes retrieval, a short source, guided and independent practice, the weekly task, a teacher-revealed MCQ checkpoint and reflection. Repeat sessions extend or revise work; project, feedback and review days remain playable rather than becoming repeated tests.

Sources use original classroom examples when the plans refer to textbook pages, photographs or packs that were not supplied. Invented numerical data and unnamed places are labeled. Original tasks and resource references remain in teacher notes. These references may need the teacher's textbook if used as extensions; the short on-screen example supports the core task. The UK graphic is explicitly a schematic location diagram, not a geographical outline map.

Stage 6 GP adaptations include short source cards, sentence starters, oral rehearsal, simple counts and bar charts, guided source evaluation and manageable community actions. Wellbeing examples concern routine preferences and evidence quality, not medical advice.

Geography covers UK location, people and settlements, weather, quality of life, connections, glaciers and landforms, the Thames, water cycle, river processes and flooding.

## Assessments

Each subject has baseline and Q1–Q4 papers. There are **ten papers**, each with **12 questions and four options**, plus separate explained answer keys. Each paper/key prints on four A4 pages. Open the paper from an assessment session or the [assessment index](../exams/).

The source plans include multi-session assessment projects and feedback. One session at each assessment point launches the MCQ paper; the other sessions keep their practical, review, presentation or reflection purpose. Quarterly papers draw on topics introduced before the test; baseline papers diagnose starting knowledge. MCQs supplement, rather than measure, actual teamwork, research and oral presentation.

7A and Grade 8 keep their existing lessons and saved progress. No student scores, names or accounts are added.
